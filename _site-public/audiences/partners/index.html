Academic collaboration brief

A teachable system with explicit boundaries

TTOD offers a local reference for teaching modern front-end architecture as one connected system. In the published Front-end II track, it is the first deliverable (Entrega 1, Units 2–6) and the product students must use and defend in the Units 1–7 mid-term—roughly the first half of the semester, before 3D, IoT, and the capstone.

Its proposed student form uses complete-example-first scaffolding: learners first trace a small working product, then take responsibility for increasingly open design and implementation tasks.

Potential academic value

  • One coherent artifact connects localization, content, islands, streaming UI, offline behavior, accessibility, testing, and performance—aligned with FE II Units 1–7.
  • Canonical content exposes provenance, rights, language, and human-review decisions.
  • Assignment seams can require authentic design without forcing students to invent the entire integration path at once.
  • Process evidence and mid-term defence of understanding can complement product behavior in an AI-assisted environment.

These are design affordances, not demonstrated learning effects.

Safeguards before classroom release

  1. The student baseline must be independently shown not to expose the rich instructor reference or its history.
  2. All starter routes and tests must pass; deliberate omissions must map to assessment criteria.
  3. English and Spanish course materials must carry equivalent outcomes, timing, and research boundaries.
  4. Local operation must not require private infrastructure or a cloud account.
  5. Research participation, if later proposed, must remain distinct from course access and grading.

Decisions invited

Partners can review curricular fit, accessibility expectations, workload, assessment validity, bilingual parity, ethics readiness, and whether a later research protocol merits development. No institutional endorsement or approval is implied by the current prototype.
