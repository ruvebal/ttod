# Assignment — Team 2, Task 10: Document one real design decision for the oral defense

**Seam:** graph · **Team:** 2 · **Task:** 10 of ~10
**Area(s):** Process · **Verb served:** question

For the Knowledge Graph seam, this task requires you to articulate a specific trade-off inherent to visualizing complex data structures. You must document a concrete decision you made regarding the graph’s layout algorithm (e.g., choosing a force-directed simulation over a static hierarchical tree), a filter interaction pattern (e.g., how users isolate specific nodes without losing context), or an accessibility compromise (e.g., how you exposed non-visual graph relationships to screen readers). Your documentation must explicitly name the alternative approach you considered and the specific technical or user-experience reason you rejected it, ensuring your defense rests on reasoned engineering choices rather than accidental outcomes.

See the shared explanation: docs/public/teaching/tasks.md#the-three-recurring-tasks

> "The best interface is no interface."
> — TTOD `ux-002`, *ux*

In the context of graph visualization, this reminds you that the most defensible design decision is often the one that reduces cognitive load by hiding complexity until the user explicitly requests it.
